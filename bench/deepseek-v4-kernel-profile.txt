nsys profile of the single-node Q2 arm, same method as bench/deepseek-v4-profile-two-node.txt
(spark1 only; `nsys profile --cuda-graph-trace=node`, one 512-token generation, 34.9 tok/s under the
profiler against 38.8 without it).

--- the GPU is busy here, unlike on the layer split ---

| | two-node UD-Q4_K_XL layer split | single-node UD-Q2_K_XL |
|---|---|---|
| GPU busy over the decode | 37.4 % (client) / 41.1 % (server) | **86.1 %** |
| kernel time in the window | 8.35 s of 22 s | 13.27 s of 15 s |

So on one node the engine keeps the GPU fed; the two-node arm's low occupancy was the layer split
serialising the machines, not a shortage of work.

Two facts worth keeping:

- the expert matvecs are already near the memory limit: one `mul_mat_vec_q_moe` call is about
  140 MB of IQ2 weights across the batch's ~28 distinct experts in 646 us, which is roughly 215 GB/s,
  near this part's 273 GB/s. Note also that Unsloth's `UD-Q2_K_XL` puts IQ2_XS/IQ2_S in the experts,
  not Q2_K;
- the shortfall is elsewhere. Across the whole pass the kernels move the ~19.35 GB of active weights
  in ~122 ms of kernel time, which is 159 GB/s, or 58 % of peak. The 23 % spent on 34344 small Q8_0
  matvecs (89 us each) is where the efficiency goes: those are the per-layer projections, small enough
  at a five-token batch to be memory-latency bound rather than bandwidth bound, the same effect that
  shows as 79 GB/s at one token per pass and 165 GB/s at five.

--- the structural difference from the reference ---

The reference serves this model at 63.1 tok/s with tensor parallelism across both Sparks, so both
machines work on the same five or so verified tokens at once. This arm uses one machine. That is the
whole of the 1.6x, and it is why closing it needs parallelism rather than more tuning:

- tensor parallelism on this model would halve the per-node traffic to about 1.9 GB per token, around
  12 ms at 165 GB/s, which fits the 74 ms a pass has at the reference's rate;
- what does not fit is the coordination. This fork spends about 74 ms per pass on it (~106 collectives
  at ~0.7 ms, measured on the UD-Q4_K_XL arm) and upstream's async design measures 50.6 ms per token
  without a draft, so roughly 30 ms per pass. The budget is about 15 ms per pass.

So the remaining work is the collective path, not the model, the quant, or the serving flags. The
measured options are: our synchronous NCCL-over-RPC (worst), upstream PR #26610's fire-and-forget
server-to-server design (about 2x better than ours on this hardware), and what vLLM already does
(both nodes on every token with the collectives overlapped, which is the 63.1 this is compared with).
